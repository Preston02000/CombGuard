Gen 1HiveMind

Where it
started.

Our first prototype, built by hand, piece by piece. It proved the idea and showed us what the real thing would need.

Step 1 · Prototype

The first circuit.

Before any case or final code, we had to prove the idea could work at all.

  • An Arduino Nano read the sensors and saved every reading to a microSD card.
  • A piezo sensor and a small amplifier listened for the colony's sound.
  • A breadboard let us rewire and test quickly.
Step 2 · CAD

Designing the case.

Next, we modeled an enclosure around the electronics.

  • Sized around the board, battery and sensor connections.
  • Several rounds of measuring, printing and adjusting.
  • Sealing and airflow became the big open questions.
Step 3 · Soldering

From breadboard to board.

Breadboard wires can shake loose, so the circuit moved onto a hand-soldered board.

  • Sturdier connections that handle heat and movement.
  • Small enough to fit inside the printed case.
Step 4 · Enclosure

The first case.

Our first 3D-printed enclosure, from when the project was still called HiveMind.

  • The name was printed right into the lid.
  • It taught us how to design parts that print cleanly and assemble easily.
Step 5 · Refined

Renamed CombGuard.

A darker, cleaner print with the honeycomb mark, and a new name.

  • A finished look for a device that sits out in the field.
  • The starting point for Gen 2.
LessonsWhat Gen 1 taught us

Three things Gen 2 had to fix.

Data has to travel.

Saving readings to a card means walking out to the hive to see them. Gen 2 sends them over Wi-Fi.

Power has to last.

Batteries alone run out. Gen 2 adds a solar panel and a charger made for it.

The field is harsh.

Sun, rain and humidity are hard on electronics. Gen 2 gets a sealed, UV-resistant case.
